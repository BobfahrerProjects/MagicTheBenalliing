# Her Majesty's Cats

*A 60-card Mardu monarch deck, built for duels, entirely from cards already in
the collection.*

**Colours:** white / black, splashing red · **Format:** 60-card constructed
(4-of rule, no commander, no sideboard) · **19 of the 35 spells have a cat in the
art.**

---

## The plan, in one sentence

Become the monarch, make it impossible to take the crown back, and let the extra
card every turn bury them.

The monarch is an emblem, not a card: while you are the monarch you **draw an
extra card at the end of your turn**, and whoever deals combat damage to you
steals it. So the deck is built to make attacking you a terrible idea. Windborn
Muse and Ghostly Prison charge {2} per attacker. Fifteen pieces of spot removal
and four sweepers clear anything that could connect. Settle the Wreckage exiles
the whole team the one time they commit to a swing.

Then Court of Grace makes a 4/4 Angel every single upkeep and the game ends.

## How to play it

**Keep a hand with two lands and two answers.** This deck does not need a fast
start; it needs to still be alive on turn four. A hand of five lands and no
spells is a mulligan, a hand of one land is a mulligan, almost everything else
is fine.

**Do not use removal on the first thing you see.** Their best card is coming.
Kill what actually threatens you — the thing with evasion, the thing that makes
more things, the thing that would take the crown.

**Take the crown as early as it is safe.** Court of Grace on turn four with a
board they cannot attack through is the whole game. Queen Marchesa is a 3/3
deathtouch blocker the turn she lands, which usually makes the crown safe by
herself.

**Fell the Mighty is a trick, not a wrath.** Target your own 2/2 and everything
bigger on the table dies. Their 5/5 dies, your cats live.

**Ajani's Response costs 2, not 5,** if the creature is tapped. Hold it for when
they attack or tap a creature for an ability.

**Settle the Wreckage wants to be a surprise.** Leave four mana up and look
unafraid. If they swing with everything to steal the crown, the game is usually
over on the spot.

**Sheltered by Ghosts answers what nothing else can.** It exiles *any* nonland
permanent — an enchantment, an artifact, a planeswalker — and the cat wearing it
gets lifelink and ward. Put it on Glittering Lion for a creature that cannot be
damaged, cannot be cheaply targeted, and gains life every hit.

### Cards that might trip you up

- **The One Ring:** the protection is only until your next turn, and you lose one
  life per burden counter each upkeep. Draw greedily, but count the life.
- **Chaos Warp** gives them a random permanent off the top. It is still the only
  card in the deck that can answer an enchantment.
- **Bitter Triumph** costs a card or 3 life on top of its mana. Pay life when
  you are healthy; discard a spare land when you are not.

## Provenance

Every card comes from a binder in the current ManaBox export. Nothing is taken
from a deck, from a wishlist, or from the `Necron Dynasties` / `Commander Hazel`
binders, which are staging for existing decks.

**Proxies: 21 of the 60 cards** — 15 lands, and six spells (Swords to Plowshares
×3, Path to Exile, The One Ring, Felidar Sovereign). The other 39 are real cards
you own. Proxy slots are recorded with `status: "proxy"` so they consume no real
copy and cannot contest another deck.

Three cards are the **last spare copy** of something another deck plan also
claims — Bitter Triumph and Sheoldred's Edict (Necron Dynasties) and Chaos Warp
(Rootha). Nothing is over-allocated: in each case the other deck's copy is
already sitting in its own binder. But there is no slack left on those three.

## Checking it

Re-run this whenever the collection changes — it re-reads the live database, so
it will tell you if a card has since been claimed by another deck:

```bash
python3 tools/verify_deck.py data/decks/mardu-cats.json
```

It checks thirteen things, including two that only the collection manager's own
parser can answer: that the ManaBox CSV parses, and that its proxy fingerprint is
unanimous. (Condition, misprint and price must *all* say proxy. If they disagree,
a ManaBox import stops and asks rather than guessing — which is the correct
behaviour, and which an export with a blank price instead of a zero will trigger.)

Every one of those checks is shown failing on a deliberately broken deck by:

```bash
python3 tools/test_verify_deck.py
```

A check that has never failed is not evidence.

## Possible swaps

- **Ruinous Ultimatum** (cat art, in `ToSort`) destroys every nonland permanent
  they control. It costs {R}{R}{W}{W}{W}{B}{B}, which ten red sources will not
  reliably produce — add Sacred Foundry ×2 and Blood Crypt ×2 (both spare in the
  proxy binder) if you want it.
- **Skyhunter Prowler / Patrol / Skirmisher / Cub** are four more real Cat
  Knights. They are below rate for a deck at this power level, but they are the
  most *cat* the collection has, and swapping them for Zoraline, Seize the
  Spoils and an Antiquities on the Loose costs less than it looks.
- **Smothering Tithe ×2** (proxy) if Ben's deck draws a lot of extra cards.
